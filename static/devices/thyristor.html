Equations and model

    

      
    
        
  • Two-transistor modelIA = (αnpn · IG + ICO) / (1 − (αpnp + αnpn))The four layers act as a pnp and an npn transistor, each feeding the other's base. Their gains α rise with current. When the sum reaches 1 the denominator vanishes and the device latches on. ICO is the leakage of the middle junction.
    • 
        
  • Load lineVS = VAK + IA · RThe operating point is where this line meets the branch the thyristor is on.
    • 
        
  • Forward blockingIA ≈ Idrm · VAK / VdrmIt turns on when VAK reaches the breakover voltage VBO = Vdrm · (1 − IG / Igt), which gate current pulls down to about 1 V.
    • 
        
  • ConductingVAK = VT0 + IA · (Vtm − VT0) / ItmA threshold of VT0 = 0.9 V plus a small resistance, passing through Vtm at Itm. It stays on, with or without gate current, while IA ≥ Ih.
    • 
        
  • Reverse blockingIA ≈ −Idrm · |VAK| / VrrmGate current has no effect in this direction.
    • 
        
  • Reverse breakdown|VAK| > VrrmThe current is limited only by the load. A real device is usually destroyed here.
    • 
      

      
    

    

  


  
Model: a static behavioural model with the parameter names of the PSpice SCR subcircuit. Blocking is a small linear leakage, breakover falls linearly with gate current, and the on state is a 0.9 V threshold plus a resistance. Switching times, dV/dt triggering, the gate voltage and the difference between latching and holding current are left out. The thyristor remembers whether it is on, so the same slider settings can give two different states depending on how you got there. Depletion widths and carrier numbers in the cross-section are sketches; the readouts and the curve use the model.
