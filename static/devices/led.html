Equations and model

    

      
    
        
  • Diode equationID = IS · (e(VD − ID · RS) / (N · VT) − 1)VT = kT/q = 25.85 mV at 27 °C, and ID · RS is the drop in the series resistance. A wide band gap makes IS extremely small, which pushes the knee up to a voltage near EG.
    • 
        
  • Colourλ = h · c / EG ≈ 1240 nm / EGEach recombination releases about the band-gap energy EG (in eV) as one photon, so the material fixes the wavelength.
    • 
        
  • Light outputPlight = η · EG · IDLight output is proportional to current, not voltage. η is the fraction of electrons that produce a photon which escapes, taken here as 20%.
    • 
        
  • OffID = 0
    • 
        
  • Below the kneeID ≈ IS · e(VD − ID · RS) / (N · VT)Too little current to see any light.
    • 
        
  • EmittingID ≈ IS · e(VD − ID · RS) / (N · VT)Above the knee the series resistance limits how fast the current rises, so in practice an LED is driven from a current source or through a resistor.

Model: the SPICE diode equation at 300 K with a series resistance and the parameters above. Choosing a material sets EG, RS and an IS that gives 20 mA at a junction voltage 0.1 V below the band gap. Light output assumes that 20% of the electrons yield a photon that escapes, whatever the current. No reverse breakdown, no heating and no efficiency droop. The emitted colour is computed from the band gap and is only an approximation of what the eye would see. Carrier and photon numbers are compressed; the readouts and the curve use the equations.
